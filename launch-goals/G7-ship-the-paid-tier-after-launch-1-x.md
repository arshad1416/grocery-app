**The design question you must settle with the owner before writing any IAP code.** PantryRun has no user accounts — no signup, no email, no password. Identity is a per-device keypair in secure storage (`GroceryApp/src/identity/device.ts`), and the relay has no server-side notion of a user. But StoreKit and Play Billing both restore purchases by *store account* (Apple ID / Google account), while the product's unit is the **family**: a household of four devices has no PantryRun-side concept of "this family paid." Three options:

1. **Per store account.** Each member buys their own. Simplest, most honest to how the stores actually work, and Apple Family Sharing can be enabled on the IAP to soften it. It fights the shared-family premise the product is built on.
2. **One purchase unlocks the family.** Write an entitlement record into the existing family Yjs document so it syncs end-to-end encrypted to every device, backed by a local table so it survives restart. This fits the architecture — the family already shares a CRDT and a master key — and needs no new server. It is forgeable by a modified client, but the monetization decision already accepts that: the repository is public, client-side gating is bypassable by building from source, and honest-user monetization is the deliberate stance.
3. **Relay-validated entitlement.** The relay validates receipts and vouches for the family. Most robust, but it puts a user-identifying record on a relay that is deliberately zero-knowledge and self-hostable. Do not choose this without a strong reason.

The owner leans toward (2) with receipt validation at purchase time, **but you must bring the tradeoff to the owner and get an explicit decision before building.** The reason this matters is that option (2) as literally described does not work today: the entitlement would not survive an app restart. Confirm this by reading `GroceryApp/src/sync/yjs-adapter.ts` and checking that nothing writes the encoded document state to storage.
